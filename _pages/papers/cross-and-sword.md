abstract: "Did Benedictine control of land generate local conflict in medieval England? We build a manor-level panel from the Domesday Book of 1086, covering more than 18,000 manors, of which about 7.3 percent were held by Benedictine monasteries as Tenants-in-Chief. We link each manor to geocoded events from the Historical Conflict Event Dataset for 1300–1700, and exploit the Dissolution of the Monasteries (1536–38) in a difference-in-differences design. After the Dissolution, conflict within 5 km of Benedictine manors fell relative to secular manors. The decline is stronger in high-value and fortified Benedictine manors; a placebo on non-Benedictine ecclesiastical manors shows no comparable effect."
